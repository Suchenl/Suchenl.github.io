Where Are We Going?

A Systematic Analysis of ICLR 2027 Submissions

When submissions double in a year, do the safeguards of peer review keep pace? Only partly. The growth is mostly new work rather than recycled ICLR rejects, and verbatim copying is essentially absent among the pairs our retrieval can surface. But anonymity is nominal for the % of submissions with a same-title arXiv preprint, and the submissions that full-text comparison confirms as duplicates of published or concurrently reviewed work were still under review at our snapshot.

RQ1 · Where does the growth come from?

New directions, not recycled rejects

About ICLR 2026 rejects reappear at ICLR 2027, against additional submissions, so ICLR's own rejects explain little of the growth. Papers rejected elsewhere, notably at NeurIPS 2026 shortly before the ICLR deadline, are invisible to us and may contribute more. The topical mix, however, shifted sharply.

RQ2 · Does the growth bring integrity problems?

Copying is rare; paraphrase is the open problem

We audit every submission against conference and arXiv abstracts with a funnel that runs from cheap, high-recall signals to expensive, high-precision ones, and calibrate each stage against positive and negative controls.

The audit funnel

Each stage is cheaper per item than the next and removes most of the remaining candidates. Hover or tap a stage.

    The full-text stage covers grade A/B leads from all sources (titles, arXiv metadata and abstracts), not only the grey zone, so it is not a strict subset of the stage above it. Counts are pairs until the full-text stage and submissions from there on.

    Verbatim reuse

    Essentially absent

    No submission reproduces the abstract of a published paper under a new title, even with a relaxed threshold. Re-titled matches are mostly benign: re-titled versions of the authors' own arXiv preprints, resubmissions of papers rejected elsewhere, and pairs of ICLR 2027 submissions with identical abstracts.

    Grey zone

    Large, and mostly legitimate

    pairs have similar meaning but different words ( against published work, within ICLR 2027, against arXiv). The second LLM pass keeps only pairs scored as "substantially the same work" against published papers, plus scored as extensions.

    Full text

    Separates true duplicates

    Reference lists, figures and runs of result numbers are hard to disguise by rewriting. All random same-area control pairs are near zero on every signal (maximum reference overlap %), and all submissions compared with their own arXiv versions are classified as the same paper.

    Anonymity, PDF checks, and what happens after rejection

    The submitted PDFs themselves leak little. Simple rules for hidden text (invisible render mode, white fill, sub-1 pt fonts, off-page text) and injection phrases fire on several percent of PDFs, but on inspection almost all hits are benign: white labels on coloured figures, embedded formula data, and papers about prompt injection. The strict rules, which require that nothing is drawn beneath the hidden text or that off-page text addresses reviewers or LLMs, find no case in the sample. % of submissions include a statement on LLM usage.

We find no evidence of an excess of fabricated references in ICLR 2027 submissions.

    Caveat This is not a measure of review randomness. Consistency experiments measure how often two committees disagree on the same version of a paper; our measure also includes revision between venues and counts only papers whose authors chose to resubmit. It cannot separate reviewer disagreement from improvement prompted by the first round of reviews. It shows only that, for borderline papers, an ICLR rejection is often not the paper's final outcome.
    Recommendations for organisers

    Checks that can run at submission time

    Organisers, who unlike outside researchers have access to author identities and the full PDFs, can run such a pipeline at negligible cost. Everything in this study ran on a single node with open-weight models.

    1. Machines rank, humans decide. Limit automatic desk rejection to objectively verifiable cases, such as full-text identity with a published paper.
    2. Use signals that paraphrase cannot hide. Reference-list overlap, image hashes and result-number fingerprints separate true duplicates from same-topic work far better than abstract similarity.
    3. Author overlap is the key triage variable. With overlap, a duplicate is a policy violation; without it, a duplicate is suspected plagiarism.
    4. Free signals are unused. arXiv comments that declare concurrent review or prior acceptance are zero-cost leads that no venue checks systematically.
    5. Exchange fingerprints across venues. Venues with overlapping review periods can exchange MinHash signatures or quantised embeddings through a trusted third party, without exchanging confidential text.
    6. Check PDFs at submission. Hidden text, injection phrases and identifying metadata, and reference resolution against DBLP, arXiv and Crossref.

    With a two-round LLM funnel, the number of pairs that need human review at ICLR scale is on the order of one hundred.

    Ethics & data

    Public data, aggregate results

    Public data only

    Every analysis uses only information that any logged-in OpenReview user, or any visitor to arXiv, DBLP and the conference websites, could see during the review period. Submission PDFs were downloaded through the public OpenReview API at its published rate limits.

    Nothing restricted to a role

    The author serves as an ICLR 2027 reviewer. We used no information visible only to reviewers, area chairs or program chairs: not the identities or size of the reviewer pool, assignments, bids, reviews, discussions or author identities.

    Aggregates only

    This page and the paper report aggregate statistics only. No submission is identified, and no title, number or excerpt of any flagged submission is shown.

    Leads are not findings

    Integrity leads come from automated signals with known error rates and have not been adjudicated. We report aggregates only and do not identify any submission, here or in the downloadable data.
